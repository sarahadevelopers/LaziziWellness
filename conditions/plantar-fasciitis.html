What is Plantar Fasciitis?

Plantar fasciitis is the inflammation of the plantar fascia—a thick band of tissue that runs across the bottom of your foot, connecting your heel bone to your toes. It acts as a shock absorber and supports the arch of your foot. When this tissue becomes overstretched or overloaded, it causes micro-tears and inflammation, leading to pain.

Common Symptoms of Plantar Fasciitis

  • Heel pain: Sharp, stabbing pain in the heel, especially with the first steps in the morning
  • Arch pain: Discomfort along the arch of the foot
  • Stiffness: Tightness in the foot and ankle
  • Pain after inactivity: Discomfort after long periods of sitting
  • Pain with prolonged standing: Increases with time on your feet
  • Pain after exercise: Especially with high-impact activities

Who is at Risk?

  • Runners and athletes
  • People who are overweight
  • Those with flat feet or high arches
  • People who wear unsupportive shoes
  • Office workers who stand for long periods
  • Pregnant women (due to weight gain and hormonal changes)

When Should You See a Doctor?

Consult a healthcare professional immediately if you experience:

  • Numbness or tingling in the foot
  • Significant swelling or redness

  • Signs of infection (fever, warmth, redness)

Tips for Managing Plantar Fasciitis at Home

  • Stretch your calves: Wall stretches and downward dog yoga pose
  • Ice your feet: Roll a frozen water bottle under your foot
  • Wear supportive shoes: Avoid flat, unsupportive footwear
  • Use orthotics: Custom or over-the-counter arch supports
  • Rest: Avoid high-impact activities during flare-ups
  • Maintain a healthy weight: Reduce stress on your feet
