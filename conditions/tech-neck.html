If you spend hours hunched over a laptop or smartphone—and let's be honest, most of us in Seattle do—you're probably familiar with the nagging ache in your neck and shoulders. That's tech-neck, and it's one of the most common conditions we treat at Lazizi® Wellness.

Tech-neck (also called forward head posture or cervical kyphosis) occurs when your head sits forward of your shoulders, putting up to 60 pounds of extra pressure on your cervical spine. Over time, this can lead to chronic pain, tension headaches, and nerve issues.

Important: Tech-neck can lead to more serious conditions if left untreated, including herniated discs, chronic migraines, and permanent postural changes. Early intervention with massage therapy is highly effective.

What is Tech-Neck?

Tech-neck is a modern condition caused by prolonged forward head posture—the position most people adopt when using phones, laptops, and tablets. The human head weighs about 10-12 pounds in neutral alignment. For every inch your head moves forward, the effective weight on your cervical spine increases by 10 pounds. At 3 inches forward, your neck is supporting 30 pounds of extra weight.

Common Symptoms of Tech-Neck

  • Neck pain and stiffness: Especially at the base of the skull
  • Upper back and shoulder pain: Often between the shoulder blades
  • Tension headaches: Starting at the back of the head
  • Numbness or tingling: In the hands or fingers
  • Restricted breathing: Shallow breaths due to chest compression
  • Reduced range of motion: Difficulty turning your head
  • Fatigue: Muscles working overtime to support your head

Who is at Risk?

  • Tech workers: Software engineers, developers, IT professionals
  • Remote workers: Anyone working from home without proper ergonomics
  • Students: Long hours studying with poor posture
  • Gamers: Extended hours in front of screens
  • Anyone who spends more than 4 hours daily on devices

When Should You See a Doctor for Neck Pain?

Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

  • Severe pain after an injury or accident
  • Numbness or tingling that spreads down your arms
  • Loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Pain that persists despite rest and home care
  • Difficulty walking or loss of coordination

Tips for Managing Tech-Neck at Home

  • Ergonomic setup: Keep your screen at eye level
  • Take breaks: Follow the 20-20-20 rule (20 minutes, look 20 feet away, for 20 seconds)
  • Stretch: Chin tucks, shoulder rolls, and chest stretches
  • Use a supportive chair: Good lumbar support
  • Strengthen: Upper back exercises like rows and face pulls
  • Regular massage: Monthly maintenance sessions

What type of massage is best for tech-neck?

Can tech-neck cause headaches?

Absolutely. Tech-neck is one of the leading causes of tension headaches. The muscles at the base of the skull become tight and can refer pain to the head.

When should I see a doctor for neck pain?

See a doctor immediately if you experience severe pain after an injury, numbness or tingling that spreads, loss of bladder or bowel control, or pain that persists despite rest and home care.
