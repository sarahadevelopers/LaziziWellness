Why Does Pregnancy Cause Back Pain?

Back pain during pregnancy is incredibly common—affecting up to 70% of expecting mothers. Here's why it happens:

  • Weight gain: The added weight shifts your center of gravity forward
  • Hormonal changes: Relaxin loosens ligaments, affecting stability
  • Postural changes: Your pelvis tilts forward to accommodate the baby
  • Muscle strain: Your back muscles work harder to support the extra weight
  • Stress: Emotional and physical stress can increase muscle tension

Common Types of Pregnancy Back Pain

  • Lower back pain: The most common type, felt in the lumbar region
  • Sciatica: Shooting pain down the leg from nerve compression
  • Pelvic pain: Discomfort in the pelvis or SI joint
  • Round ligament pain: Sharp pain in the lower abdomen or groin
  • Middle back pain: Tension in the thoracic region

How Prenatal Massage Can Help with Pregnancy Back Pain

Prenatal massage can be a safe and effective way to help relieve back pain during pregnancy when performed by a trained therapist. Here's how it may help:

  • Relieves muscle tension: Releases tight back, neck, and shoulder muscles
  • Improves circulation: Increases blood flow, reducing swelling and promoting healing
  • Reduces stress hormones: Lowers cortisol and promotes relaxation
  • Supports better sleep: Relaxation improves sleep quality
  • Decreases nerve compression: Gentle techniques relieve sciatic pain
  • Prepares for labor: Relaxation techniques can help with delivery

Our Prenatal Massage Approach

Our WA licensed therapists use a combination of techniques tailored to your specific needs and trimester:

  • Side-Lying Positioning: Comfortable side-lying position with pregnancy pillows
  • Gentle Pressure: Light to medium pressure tailored to your comfort
  • Myofascial Release: Gentle stretching of tight fascia
  • Swedish Techniques: Long, flowing strokes for relaxation
  • Safe Oils: Natural, pregnancy-safe products
  • Customized Pressure: We avoid deep pressure on the lower back and certain acupressure points

When to Start Prenatal Massage

  • First Trimester (1-12 weeks): Light massage with doctor's approval
  • Second Trimester (13-27 weeks): Most therapists recommend starting now
  • Third Trimester (28-40 weeks): Many women find weekly sessions helpful
  • Postpartum: Gentle massage can aid recovery after birth

Who Can Benefit?

  • Expecting mothers in any trimester (with doctor approval)
  • Women experiencing lower back pain or sciatica
  • Mothers dealing with stress, anxiety, or sleep issues
  • Anyone wanting to prepare physically for labor
  • Postpartum mothers recovering from birth
  • Women with high-risk pregnancies (consult your doctor first)

When to Avoid Prenatal Massage

Consult your healthcare provider before booking if you have:

  • High blood pressure or preeclampsia
  • Severe swelling or edema
  • History of preterm labor
  • Placenta previa or other placental conditions
  • Any condition your doctor advises against

Frequently Asked Questions

Is prenatal massage safe during pregnancy?

Yes, with your doctor's approval and a trained therapist. Our therapists follow strict prenatal massage protocols and use safe positioning.

When should I start prenatal massage?

Most therapists recommend starting in the second trimester (around 13-14 weeks). Always check with your doctor first.

Do you offer mobile prenatal massage in Seattle?

Yes! We bring the table and pregnancy pillows to your Seattle or Bellevue home. Perfect for expecting mothers who want to avoid travel.

What areas do you avoid during prenatal massage?

We avoid deep pressure on the lower back, abdomen, and certain acupressure points. We always use professional draping and positioning.

Can prenatal massage help with sciatica during pregnancy?

Yes! Our therapists are trained to safely relieve sciatic pain during pregnancy using gentle techniques that avoid pressure on the nerve.

Is it safe to have a massage in the third trimester?

Yes, with your doctor's approval. Many women find weekly sessions in the third trimester very helpful for pain and stress relief.

When should I avoid prenatal massage?

Avoid massage if you have high blood pressure, preeclampsia, severe swelling, or any condition your doctor advises against. Always consult your healthcare provider first.
