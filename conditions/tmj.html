If you experience jaw pain, clicking, facial tension, or frequent tension headaches, you may be dealing with temporomandibular joint disorder (TMJ / TMD). Symptoms vary widely and can be driven by a mix of muscular tension, joint issues, dental factors, stress and clenching habits.

At Lazizi® Wellness, we offer focused massage sessions for the muscles around the jaw, temples, neck and shoulders — the areas most commonly associated with jaw-muscle tension. Our sessions are designed to complement — not replace — evaluation and care from your dentist, physician, or a TMD specialist.

Can massage help with TMJ?

Massage may help ease muscle tension around the jaw, temples, neck and shoulders for some people, and some clients find it a useful complement to broader care. It is not a treatment for the joint itself and does not replace dental or medical evaluation. If your symptoms are severe, worsening, or involve jaw locking, swelling, or bite changes, see a dentist, TMD specialist or physician before relying on massage.

What Is TMJ Disorder?

TMJ disorders involve the temporomandibular joint (which connects your jaw to your skull) and/or the muscles that move the jaw. Symptoms can include jaw pain or stiffness, clicking or popping, headaches in the temples, ear discomfort, facial muscle fatigue, and difficulty chewing. Causes are often mixed, and diagnosis typically requires a dentist or physician.

For a fuller explanation of symptoms, causes and self-care, see our TMJ Massage Guide.

Common Symptoms Clients Report

  • Jaw pain, stiffness or tightness
  • Clicking, popping or grinding sounds when opening the mouth
  • Tension headaches, especially in the temples
  • Facial muscle fatigue or tightness
  • Neck and shoulder tension
  • Ear discomfort or a feeling of fullness
  • Difficulty chewing or biting

Optional intraoral massage

Intraoral massage involves gentle work inside the mouth to address the pterygoid and masseter muscles, which can be difficult to reach externally. It is offered as an optional part of some TMJ-focused sessions.

  • Consent-based: We explain what intraoral work involves before we start. You can decline it at any time, and external-only sessions are always available.
  • Hygiene: We use gloves, fresh linens and standard hygiene protocols for intraoral work.
  • When a referral may be appropriate: If your symptoms involve joint degeneration, bite changes, or haven't improved with conservative care, we recommend coordinating with a dentist, TMD specialist or your physician. Intraoral massage is not a substitute for dental or medical evaluation of the joint itself.

What Massage Can and Cannot Do

Massage may help with… Easing muscle tightness in the jaw, temples, neck and shoulders, supporting relaxation, and reducing the muscular component some people feel with jaw-related tension.
Massage is not… A treatment for joint degeneration, bite problems, or the underlying cause of a TMJ disorder. It is not a substitute for dental or medical evaluation.

When to See a Doctor or Dentist

Seek prompt assessment from a healthcare professional if you experience any of the following:

  • Severe jaw pain, or pain that isn't improving with home care
  • Difficulty opening or closing your mouth
  • Your jaw locks in an open or closed position
  • Significant swelling, warmth or redness around the jaw
  • Fever
  • Recent injury or trauma to the jaw
  • Changes in your bite

A dentist, TMD specialist or physician can evaluate the joint itself and coordinate care.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is TMJ massage painful?

It may feel mildly uncomfortable if the muscles are very tight, but pressure is always adjusted to your comfort. Some clients find jaw work deeply relaxing; others prefer very light pressure. You can decline any area at any time, including intraoral work.

How many sessions will I need?

Response varies from person to person. Some clients feel more comfortable after one or two sessions, while others use massage as periodic maintenance alongside stress management and any care recommended by their dentist or physician. There is no fixed protocol.

Do you offer mobile TMJ massage in Seattle?

Yes. We bring the table, linens and oils to your home or office in Seattle, Bellevue and surrounding King County areas. Mobile sessions include a $40 travel fee.

Can massage help with TMJ related to stress?

Stress and clenching may contribute to jaw tension for some people. Massage may help ease muscle tightness around the jaw, temples, neck and shoulders, and some clients find it a useful complement to stress-management approaches. It is not a guaranteed treatment for TMJ disorders.

When should I see a doctor or dentist for TMJ pain?

Seek prompt assessment if you have severe jaw pain, difficulty opening or closing your mouth, jaw locking open or closed, significant swelling, fever, or ongoing pain that isn't improving with home care. A dentist, TMD specialist or your physician can evaluate the joint itself and coordinate care.

Can TMJ massage help with headaches?

Some people with tension-type headaches related to jaw or neck tightness find that massage helps ease the muscular component. Massage is not a treatment for headache disorders. If headaches are new, severe, or worsening, see a healthcare provider.

This information is for educational purposes and does not constitute medical advice. Massage should not replace dental or medical care for TMJ disorders or any other condition. Consult a dentist, TMD specialist or other qualified healthcare provider for diagnosis and treatment.
